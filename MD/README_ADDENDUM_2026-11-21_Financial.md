# Financial losses and use of emergency reserves
## README addendum · 21 November 2026

**Personal account of Caleb Weddington for the Harvey Labs financial archive.** The following first-person narrative is an editorial rendering of my account. The original message follows in full. Balances, limits, credit scores, timing and spending estimates are my recollections unless a separate source is identified. My descriptions of the underlying events and their effects reflect my experience and my understanding of cause.

### Before December 2024

I was saving toward a home. My target was a modest purchase in 2026 or, at the latest, 2027, with an estimated down payment of $35,000–$50,000. Establishing a relationship with Navy Federal Credit Union (NFCU), including an early informal consultation, was part of that plan. I recall promptly repaying a smaller personal loan and using a starter card to build credit. I kept about four cards at zero or well below 10% utilization and only rarely approached 30%, paying balances down deliberately. I recall a credit score above 750 around December 2024. I had no auto loan. I kept a student loan of roughly $10,000 on a payment schedule under $100 per month, partly to maintain a longer credit history. I estimate the balance remains near $10,000.

I maintained a six-month cash emergency reserve of approximately $15,000–$17,500 and a separate taxable Fidelity portfolio earmarked for the down payment. I recall about $17,500 in the mortgage portfolio before the emergency. From an annual salary of approximately $86,500, I budgeted on the assumption that taxes and other payroll deductions took about 35%. Once I covered rent, food, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, studio software and my other regular expenses, I generally invested the surplus. That included recurring contributions of $200 or more, my bonus when available and occasional additional deposits of about $1,000–$2,000. My usual food purchases, including Chipotle, and nicotine pouches already formed part of my ordinary spending.

### December 2024 through August 2026

While I was still employed, the circumstances described in the README became a personal emergency for me. I continued paying the base rent and maintained familiar day-to-day habits. I directed money that had been available for the down payment toward expenses I associated with restoring my digital security and addressing the ongoing audio and privacy concerns. These included an additional T-Mobile expense, recurring service costs, materials and travel for my efforts to understand what I was experiencing. Jiu-jitsu dues eventually stopped. Around May 2025 I stopped my regular Fidelity contributions. I recall exhausting the original six-month cash reserve during 2025 while also using credit. In early to mid-2026 medical bills began arriving. I associate roughly six to twelve visits with those concerns. I also recall that the bonus I anticipated in early 2026 was approximately half what I had expected, which I attributed to the disruption.

My credit cards eventually reached or approached their limits. I recall NFCU converting or raising the starter card limit to about $2,000. The other approximate limits I named were Discover $2,000, Chase Amazon $3,200, Chase Marriott $5,000, Home Depot $6,000 and Capital One $2,000. At least two cards were obtained during this period. I believe my credit score may have fallen below 560. That figure is a guess. The six listed limits describe accounts, not an account-by-account measure of what I owed or lost. I recall that my own vehicle became or remained unusable and that a newer vehicle used by the family or household accumulated roughly 15,000 miles of use I associate with the same period.

A different $10,000 NFCU personal loan concerned a family foreclosure and a property of sentimental significance. I regard that as a separate family emergency. I recall paying approximately $5,000 back promptly when I was concerned about the security of the funds at NFCU. I estimate about $4,500 remains outstanding. This loan is separate from the small loan I recall repaying while establishing credit.

### From 1 September 2026

I use 1 September as the beginning of this post-employment budgeting period. The emergency itself began while I was still working. At the transition I recall approximately $30,000 in the taxable Fidelity account. By then the down-payment fund had become my backup emergency reserve, available by selling investments and transferring cash. I recall often being able to move sale proceeds to my bank within hours. Historical withdrawal and bank records can document individual movements. The description of what a withdrawal was for comes from me unless the onward payment also appears in a record.

I estimate about $6,000 of the initial funds went toward overdue rent and card payments. My rent calculation was ten weeks at $300, or $3,000. I recall five card payments across three approximate bands: two of $600–$800, one of $400–$600 and two of $300–$400. Together these come to about $2,200–$3,000, or $5,200–$6,000 including rent. I have not assigned these five payments to specific cards. I listed six card accounts elsewhere in the same message. The figures describe the catch-up period, which helps explain why the cash outflows near the start were higher than my intended ongoing spending. I also recall a further withdrawal around October and a recent $800 withdrawal for the current month. I expected to need another transfer soon.
